# Colorado Plateau rock marks: candidates for "mark of work" glyphs

Method note: web search + HTTP status check (curl, 200 unless noted). Some facts (cairn stone counts, disk stamping look, sizes of fins) come from secondary sources or my own knowledge and are flagged "unverified". 403/404 pages are not listed as verified.

## Per-candidate

### 1. Cairn (human-made)
- What: Stacked stones marking routes across slickrock and sand where permanent signs are impractical. NPS Arches says many trails use cairns and cross open rock; cairns are "important for marking hiking routes" and visitors must not build or move them. Sources: https://www.nps.gov/thingstodo/hiking-at-arches.htm ; https://home.nps.gov/arch/planyourvisit/leave-no-trace.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/places/pothole-point.htm (Pothole Point loop follows cairns).
- Signature: shape = 3 to 5 stacked flat stones narrowing upward (stack height about 1 to 1.5x base width; typical park cairns are knee height; count/proportion unverified, from photos); tone = same as rock but with a dark shadow side and pale top; sits ON the rock, casting a short shadow.
- Draw (3 shapes): (a) base stone, wide flat trapezoid in mid-strata tone; (b) middle stone, narrower, slightly lighter; (c) cap stone, smallest, lightest. Add one 1px dark shadow wedge to the lee side, or reuse darkest strata tone.
- Type: number of stones (paper 4, patent 3, system 5, post 2) or the cap stone offset left/right; avoid colour. Draft: top stone lifted and tilted/detached (a fallen stone beside the stack), or the cairn drawn as 2 stones only with outline.
- Failure at 6-8 px: a stack of 3 horizontal bars reads as a "pagoda", a totem, a stacked-coins/layer-cake icon or a "stone-balancing / zen" wellness icon; a tall narrow one reads as a mushroom if the cap is wider than the stem (keep the cap narrower than the base).
- Caveat: NPS actively discourages visitor cairn building; the official ones are route markers. Metaphor "this is the route I walked" is good for notes/posts.

### 2. Survey benchmark disk (human-made)
- What: Brass/bronze disc about 3.5 in across with stamped text, cemented into a drilled hole (stem about 3 in in early USGS design) flush with bedrock or a summit. NGS bedrock procedure: ~2.5 cm hole, ~10 cm deep, rock recessed so the disk sits level. Sources: https://bubbajuice.github.io/benchmarks/types/usgs (secondary, early USGS design); NOAA setting procedure https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20130213214023/http://tidesandcurrents.noaa.gov/publications/SettingSurveyDiskBedrock.pdf ; NGS marks index https://www.ngs.noaa.gov/marks/descriptors.shtml ; USGS YVO article saying about 3.5 in (my fetch of usgs.gov/observatories/yvo/news/benchmarking-benchmarks failed, treat as unverified).
- Signature: shape = small circle with a centre cross/triangle and a ring of lettering; tone = warm brass, brighter than any stratum, or dull olive-bronze when weathered; sits IN the rock (flush, with a dark crescent of shadow in the recess).
- Draw (3 shapes): (a) dark recess circle (darkest rock tone), (b) disc offset 0.5px up-left in brass or light buff, (c) a tiny centre tick or triangle in the dark tone.
- Type: centre glyph (circle-dot = paper, triangle = patent, cross = system, bar = post) which matches how real disks carry triangulation-station triangles and crosses. Draft: disc drawn as outline ring only ("unset/unstamped"), or recess with no disc.
- Failure: a bright disc reads as a coin, bullet hole, bolt/rivet, radio button or a game "pickup". At 6-8 px the centre mark is nearly invisible, so type must lean on size/ring differences.
- Fit: strongest "this was set here on purpose, to say I stood here / this is a known point" meaning. "Witness posts" (small marker posts near a mark, and often a "REFERENCE MARK" disk) are a variant (flat disk + short post) but are not distinct enough at 8 px.

### 3. Petroglyphs on desert varnish (human-made, appropriation risk)
- What: Figures pecked or scraped through the dark manganese/iron varnish to expose pale rock. BLM Newspaper Rock brochure: carvings are pecked/scraped, the dark varnish gave a contrasting surface, new images layered on old. Sources: https://www.blm.gov/sites/default/files/documents/files/BLMUtah_NewspaperRock_Brochure.pdf ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_Rock ; varnish: manganese ~50-60x crustal abundance, microbial vs abiotic origin contested: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8237629
- Signature: shape = figural outlines; tone = pale on dark (a light inversion); sits IN/ON a dark vertical face, not on a horizontal bench.
- Failure: any figure drawn will read as a pictograph emoji, "stick man", tribal icon. Only works on a dark vertical varnish streak.
- Appropriation: these are sacred or ancestral cultural expressions (Fremont, Ancestral Puebloan, Ute, Navajo, and Anglo layers per sources). Do NOT draw figures, spirals, bighorn, kokopelli. If used at all, use the neutral abstract version: a dark varnish streak with 1 to 3 pale pecked dots or short dashes (as tally marks), drawn as texture not symbols. Recommendation: use the varnish streak only as background/ground, not as a mark.

### 4. Carved inscriptions (human-made)
- What: Travellers' names carved into soft sandstone. NPS: El Morro has 2,000+ inscriptions, petroglyphs and pictographs; oldest Spanish carving 1605; "paso por aqui" formula; soft, friable Zuni Sandstone; carving banned by 1906 law. Sources: https://www.npshistory.com/publications/foundation-documents/elmo-fd-overview.pdf ; https://www.nps.gov/articles/nps-geodiversity-atlas-el-morro-national-monument-new-mexico.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/elmo/learn/historyculture/the-americans.htm . Register Cliff (Wyoming, soft limestone, 1840s-50s, state site): https://www.nps.gov/places/000/register-cliff-historic-site.htm
- Signature: shape = small recessed rectangular tablets (often a framed panel with scroll top) with horizontal text lines; tone = slightly darker or lighter than the cliff, with a fresh-cut pale scar; sits IN the vertical cliff face.
- Draw (3 shapes): recessed rounded rectangle (shadow tone), 1 to 2 lighter horizontal bars for text, a thin lighter top edge for the carved lip. At distance real inscriptions are just pale horizontal scratches.
- Type: number of text lines (1 to 3) and the panel's outline form. Draft: panel left blank (uncut), lines dashed.
- Failure: reads as a document icon, a list, a tombstone, or a sign. Needs a vertical face, so it only fits on the cliff face below the bench, not on the bench.
- Fit: "signed my name" is on point for papers/patents. Also best neutral human-made candidate (Spanish/American traveller signatures; no sacred content), though it is colonial history, so avoid imitating specific names.

### 5. Fins (natural)
- What: Thin parallel rock walls left by erosion along vertical joints, in Entrada Sandstone (Fiery Furnace, Devils Garden); arches form when a fin is breached. Sources: https://www.nps.gov/articles/nps-geodiversity-atlas-arches-national-park-utah.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/arch/learn/nature/geologicformations.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/parkhistory/online_books/geology/publications/bul/1393/sec8.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fin_(geology). Real fins are tall relative to width and much taller than a bench; at 6-8 px, relative proportion is my estimate.
- Signature: shape = tall narrow rectangles in a parallel row, often slightly leaning; tone = same stratum colour, one face lit, one shaded; sits ON/out of the rock as part of it.
- Draw (3 shapes): per fin a lit-face parallelogram, a shaded side face, and (for draft) a gap. Row of 1-4 fins; also a "notched" fin with a keyhole gap = arch-in-progress.
- Type: fin count or height; the ordered rows already match "rows". Draft: fin broken or with a window/notch; or low stub.
- Failure: reads as bar chart bars, books on a shelf, gravestones, or a city skyline. Not human-made; feels like terrain rather than "mine".
- Fit: strongest as terrain-consistent; weak as a "mark of work" unless the row is the data itself.

### 6. Tinajas / potholes (natural)
- What: Weathering pits that hold rain; also tanks, waterpockets, ephemeral pools; widened by slightly acidic water; millimetres to metres across, with invertebrates adapted to swings (surface up to 140 F). Sources: https://www.nps.gov/cany/learn/nature/pools.htm ; https://www.nps.gov/cany/learn/photosmultimedia/inside_potholes.htm ; https://geology.utah.gov/map-pub/survey-notes/glad-you-asked/potholes ; https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/nrca_glca_2021_tinajas.htm (from search result; not fetched).
- Signature: shape = irregular rounded ellipse with a lighter lip on the lit side; tone = dark (shadow or water), darker than bench; sits IN the bench.
- Draw (3 shapes): dark ellipse, thin pale rim crescent on the far edge, optional sky-blue/grey sliver reflection (only for "public" if a fourth tone is allowed).
- Type: size and ellipse elongation, double pot (two merged pits) etc. Draft: dry pothole = ellipse with a flat pale floor and no water highlight. Public vs draft maps nicely to wet vs dry, the best natural public/draft pair on the list.
- Failure: reads as a hole, shadow blob, "map pin shadow", button, or a hole-punch. Cannot carry type well beyond size.
- Fit: very recognisable on benches (Pothole Point), very legible as dark spots, natural not human-made.

### 7. Moki steps (human-made, appropriation risk)
- What: Alternating hand and toe holds cut into steep sandstone, usually 2-3 in deep and 3-4 in wide, associated with Ancestral Puebloan peoples; some similar steps are by later settlers; archaeologists urge visitors not to climb them. Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moki_steps (no NPS page found). "Moki" is also considered an outdated/offensive exonym for the Hopi/Puebloan peoples, which matters for naming.
- Signature: shape = short zig-zag column of small notches; tone = dark pits; sits IN a vertical face.
- Failure: reads as a ladder, a zipper, a rope bridge, a "progress track", or just noise; needs a cliff face. 
- Appropriation: these are ancestral sites, still sensitive. Avoid them as a "mark" and avoid the term "Moki" in any UI copy; if a ladder-of-notches motif is desired call it "toeholds". Recommended: skip.

### 8. Others
- Hoodoos: caprock on a softer pedestal, formed by frost wedging, Claron Formation at Bryce (NPS https://www.nps.gov/brca/learn/nature/hoodoos.htm). At 6-8 px a cap wider than the stem is exactly a mushroom: this is the reason the earlier attempt failed. Don't use.
- Balanced rock / pedestal: same mushroom failure.
- Moqui marbles (iron-oxide-shelled spherical concretions weathering out of Navajo Sandstone; Live Science https://www.livescience.com/47909-photo-gallery-fantastic-moqui-marbles.html, Commons https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:Moqui_marbles ). Small dark-brown/rust spheres scattered ON the rock. Draw: 1 dark circle + 1 lit highlight dot + optional shadow. Natural, legible at 6 px (marbles are literally a few mm to cm, so scale is wrong but visually fine), carries no sensitive meaning; but "Moqui" name carries the same exonym issue, so call them "iron concretions" or "spheres". Failure: looks like a dot-marker / bullet point / ball. Type by cluster (1,2,3 or a "doublet"), draft as a hollow/split shell (weathered half). Rank high for neutrality.
- Cryptobiotic soil: dark knobby crust on sand, NPS https://www.nps.gov/arch/learn/nature/disturbedlands.htm ; reads as noise/texture; not a mark; but a good ground texture for the bench.
- Checkerboard joints: orthogonal fracture pattern on Navajo/Entrada slickrock; texture, not a mark.
- Summit register: a metal box or jar in a cairn with a notebook, on peaks; human-made; I did not verify a Plateau-specific source. Draw as cairn + small rectangular tin. Fit is excellent conceptually (a register is literally "a record of work done/visits"), but at 7 px collapses to a cairn with a pixel on top. Variation: use as the draft/public flag (a cairn with a tin = published).
- Talus/boulder fields: scattered angular blocks at cliff base; clutter, not distinct.
- Cowboy glyphs / names: carved/painted names and brands; same as inscriptions, risk of mimicking graffiti.

## Recommendation
Use two families so both bench rows read:
- Primary mark, per work: a 2 to 5-stone cairn (stone count = type; no colour), with the "summit register" tin only on published/public items, or a dropped top stone for drafts.
- Alternative or secondary for items fixed in place (patents/shipped systems): benchmark disk flush in the bench with a different centre tick; this reads as "established point" and is highly legible because brass is brighter than the strata.
- Use potholes as a wet/dry status indicator beside the numeral if one extra cue is needed (wet = public, dry = draft); use dry vs wet without new colours by changing only the highlight.
- Keep hoodoo-like proportions out: cap must be narrower than the base for every cairn.
- Petroglyphs/Moki steps: do not use figures or notched ladders; "Moki" avoid in copy; abstract varnish streak as ground texture only. If you want a cultural mention, credit and link NPS/BLM pages rather than imitating the art.
