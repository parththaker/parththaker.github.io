# Council round 6 brief: what stands for a piece of work on the rock

You are one voice in a blind design council. Answer independently, concretely, in 1,000 to 1,500 words.

## The object
A personal research site draws a "map of work" as a plan-oblique relief in the manner of the Colorado Plateau: one plateau cut into seven flat-topped mesas, each a stack of flat terraces in muted strata colours (Hermit/Supai red at the base, Claron pink, Entrada salmon, Coconino buff at the summit; a pale jade shelf under water around the shore). Light from the lower left, flat shading, hairline rims, no textures beyond faint bedding lines on the risers. Terraces are layers of evidence from a ledger (cited, read, written, published); the summit bench is the owner's published work and the bench below it his other writing.

On those two benches, each individual work (a paper, a patent, a shipped system, a post, a note; later talks, datasets, code) needs a mark. The mark is seen only at the mesa level, where it is about 6 to 8 px across with a two-digit numeral beside it, laid out in rows along the front of the bench, oldest to newest. Clicking it opens a panel. There may be two to nine marks per bench. A `public: false` item (a draft) must look different from a public one. Material type should be distinguishable but the numeral is the primary identity.

Two attempts were rejected by the owner. First: small cylinders with a wider cap standing on the bench ("mushrooms", ugly at 6 px). Second: flat map glyphs (circle, triangle, bar) engraved into the bench, which were clean but did not feel like part of the rock; they read as UI symbols stuck onto a landscape. The owner's words: it "has to be something which is in synergy with the rest of the Canyon kinda rock theme". He wants five to seven options to choose from, drawn and compared.

## Candidates on the table (add better ones if you have them)
1. Cairn: two or three flat stones stacked, the trail marker of every slickrock route on the Plateau. Human-made, stands on the bench, reads "someone was here and left a mark".
2. Benchmark disk: a brass survey disk set flush in summit rock, the way USGS marked high points. Human-made, set in the bench, a small bright disc with a darker ring.
3. Petroglyph on varnish: a pale pecked figure on a patch of dark desert varnish, as at Newspaper Rock. Human-made, in the rock; the glyph shape can carry the type.
4. Inscription on the cliff face: a short line of carved marks on the riser below the summit, as at El Morro's Inscription Rock. Human-made, against the rock; the riser faces the viewer in plan-oblique, so the marks are legible; numerals could literally be the inscription.
5. Fin: a thin upright slab, as in the Fiery Furnace; a row of fins is a wall broken into pieces. Natural, stands on the bench; carries height, which the design has so far refused to give to items.
6. Tinaja: a weathering pothole holding dark water, set into the bench. Natural, in the rock; a dark ellipse with a lit rim.
7. Moki step: a hand-cut toe-hold notch in the riser; a column of notches is a ladder. Human-made, against the rock.
8. Hoodoo without the cap, cairn-like spire; or a balanced rock; or a boulder on the bench. Natural, stands on the bench; the first two attempts came from this family.

## Constraints
- Flat-shaded SVG, at most three tones per mark, all drawn from the existing palette roles (top tone, lit riser, shade riser, rim highlight, ink, brand red for hover and selection only).
- Readable at 6 to 8 px beside a numeral; rows of up to nine; must not read as a game piece, a pin, a chart marker or a mushroom.
- Must not introduce a second height scale that competes with the terraces. A mark may be "set in" or "lying on" the bench freely; if it stands, it must be clearly a token, not a measure.
- Type (paper, patent, system, post, note, later talk, dataset, code) must be carryable by a small variation of the same mark, and draft versus public by another variation, without new colours.
- Cultural care: petroglyphs and Moki steps are Ancestral Puebloan; say whether using their look for a personal CV is acceptable and, if used, how to keep it respectful (abstract, no figurative glyphs).
- Nothing on the mark says what the terraces already say.

## Deliver
1. Your ranking of the candidates (including any you add), with one sentence each on why.
2. For your top three: an SVG recipe (shapes, sizes in px at the mesa level, tones by palette role), how type and draft are carried, how a row of nine looks, the failure mode.
3. Your single recommendation and the one sentence the method page would use to explain it.
4. One thing not to do.
