My governing judgment is that the mark should read as a record deliberately fixed to a geological surface, not as a miniature landform. That preserves the canyon metaphor without creating another height scale. At 6–8 px, type variations should be compact codes rather than eight tiny pictograms; the numeral remains the reliable identifier.

## 1. Ranking

1. **Benchmark disk** — Best overall: a survey tablet set into summit rock is both thematically exact and exceptionally legible, while implying that the work has been located, recorded, and made part of the map.

2. **Cairn** — The strongest standing option because it represents deliberate human passage through a landscape, although it needs an asymmetric, low construction to avoid returning to the rejected mushroom silhouette.

3. **Inscription on the cliff face** — The best fully rock-integrated option because the item becomes an authored cut in the mesa itself, with the risk that a long row begins to resemble ruler ticks or interface labels.

4. **Tinaja** — Visually native to sandstone and robust at small scale, but semantically it suggests erosion or absence rather than completed intellectual work.

5. **Fin** — Clearly geological and easy to draw, but nine repeated fins create a miniature skyline and therefore compete with the terraces’ established height language.

6. **Petroglyph on varnish** — Formally effective but not appropriate as the default visual vocabulary for a personal CV because it borrows a culturally specific record-making tradition for decorative identity.

7. **Moki step** — Poor as an item mark because a row reads as a ladder or traversal device rather than separate works, with the same cultural concern as the petroglyph option.

I would remove hoodoos, balanced rocks, and generic boulders before presenting the choice. They belong to the same “small object standing on a stem or footprint” family that already produced the mushroom problem.

Petroglyph-derived marks should be used only with a meaningful cultural relationship or informed consultation. Simply making them abstract does not erase the source. If the visual principle is retained, it should be reframed as a generic **chiseled registry mark**: no copied figures, animals, spirals, handprints, ceremonial forms, or imitation varnish panel. Likewise, I would not use the historically loaded “Moki steps” as a decorative theme; “hand-and-toe holds” is more descriptive, but the underlying appropriation problem remains.

## 2. Top-three recipes

### Benchmark disk

Use a horizontal ellipse approximately **7 × 4.5 px**, respecting the bench’s plan-oblique projection rather than drawing a perfect screen-space circle.

Draw three shapes:

- A **7 × 4.5 px outer ellipse in ink**, serving as the drilled socket and dark setting seam.
- A **6 × 3.6 px inset ellipse in rim highlight**, shifted about 0.25 px toward the light source.
- A **4.8 × 2.7 px face in the bench’s top tone**, also slightly light-shifted.

This produces a thin bright metal or polished-stone rim without introducing literal brass. On hover or selection, replace the highlight rim—and optionally the numeral—with brand red; do not add red as a fourth simultaneous tone.

Carry material type with a three-position edge code cut into the front half of the rim. Left, centre, and right positions form a three-bit code, giving eight combinations for paper, patent, system, post, note, talk, dataset, and code. Each cut is a blunt rectangular interruption about **0.7–0.9 px wide**, not a symbolic icon. The distinctions will be learnable from a legend and useful for scanning, but the nearby numeral remains authoritative.

A public item has a continuous back rim. A draft has a fixed **1.5 px opening at the back-right** of the rim, always separate from the front-edge type code. The open ring should also be expressed in accessibility text and the panel; status must not depend exclusively on noticing a six-pixel gap.

Nine disks should appear as quiet, repeated registration points: marker, 1–1.5 px gap, two-digit tabular numeral, then roughly 4 px before the next group. At a group pitch of about 17–19 px, the row looks like a chain of surveyed stations rather than beads or chart points.

Its failure mode is becoming a generic data dot. The setting seam, oblique ellipse, highlighted rim, and consistent physical relation to the bench are therefore essential. If the site already uses circles as chart points or pagination indicators, this option drops substantially in rank.

### Cairn

Keep the cairn **7 px wide and no more than 5.5 px high**, with three visibly separate, irregular slabs rather than a stem and cap:

- A lower quadrilateral roughly **7 × 2.2 px**.
- A middle slab roughly **5.3 × 1.9 px**, offset 0.7 px left or right.
- A top chip roughly **3.2 × 1.6 px**, offset in the opposite direction.

Each slab has a top plane in top tone, a lower-left face in lit-riser tone, and a short right face in shade-riser tone. Use only those three tones. Avoid outlines, oval stones, a central axis, or a top slab wider than the slab below it. Those choices are what turn a cairn into a mushroom or a game pawn.

Use the same three-position type code as the benchmark, but express it as tiny square nicks in the front edge of the broad base stone. This makes type a property of the construction without changing its overall silhouette.

A public work is a settled three-stone cairn. A draft uses the same base and middle stone but places the top chip immediately beside them, lying flat on the bench. That reads as “not yet completed” without adding another colour; the clickable hit area remains identical for both states.

A row of nine should resemble a line of low trail cairns, with deliberately alternating offsets so it does not become a mechanical picket fence. The numerals should share one baseline even though the stones vary slightly. Variation must be templated—perhaps three approved arrangements rotated through the row—not procedurally random.

The failure mode is silhouette collapse: at 6 px, insufficient separation makes the stones merge into a cone, while excessive separation makes them debris. It is also the option most likely to compete with terrace height, so it needs testing in the full mesa rather than in isolation.

### Chiseled registry on the riser

Place the mark on the front riser immediately below the relevant bench. Use a shallow irregular cartouche about **7 × 5 px**, followed by the numeral on the same visual baseline.

The groove is an open, slightly skewed rectangular path in ink, approximately **0.7 px thick**. Add a duplicate highlight stroke offset **0.4 px toward the lower-left light source**, clipped so that it appears only on the illuminated lip of the cut. The existing riser colour supplies the third tone; no filled plaque is needed. Render the numeral by the same incised treatment—ink stroke with a restrained highlight edge—so it belongs to the rock rather than floating over it.

Material type is carried by up to three short chisel cuts along the cartouche’s lower edge, again using the shared three-bit code. A public work has a closed cartouche. A draft leaves the right side unfinished, with the terminal stroke stopping about 1.2 px short.

Nine marks form a restrained inscription band across the face. Give every item a fixed-width cell so that the numerals do not drift, while allowing the cartouche corners to vary by fractions of a pixel. This is the only top-three option whose row can comfortably become part of the mesa’s bedding rhythm.

Its failure mode is looking like a ruler, file index, or debug overlay. It also fails when the riser is too shallow or too dark to support the highlight-and-groove pair. The remedy is not stronger contrast; if the riser cannot hold the carving naturally, use the benchmark disk instead.

## 3. Recommendation

Choose the **benchmark disk**. It has the clearest semantic fit—surveying, location, evidence, permanence—and the lowest visual cost. It is physically integrated without impersonating a natural formation, supports status and type codes, and remains recognizable at the required size.

Method-page sentence:

> Each work is marked by a survey tablet set into its writing bench: the rim code records its material, an open rim denotes a draft, and the adjacent number opens its ledger entry.

## 4. One thing not to do

Do not turn the eight material types into eight miniature pictograms. At this scale they will either become illegible or recreate the rejected “UI symbols placed on scenery” problem; use one physical mark with a restrained, documented edge code.

**Confidence: 0.86.** I would change the recommendation if a true-size 1× raster test showed the disk’s rim and status opening collapsing, or if circular marks already have a strong unrelated meaning elsewhere on the site.
