# Round 6 synthesis (Fable): what stands for a piece of work on the rock

Voices: Sol (xhigh), Terra (high), Opus, plus a research agent that checked the Plateau precedents (cairns, benchmark disks, desert varnish and petroglyphs, El Morro inscriptions, fins, tinajas, toe-holds, concretions) against NPS and USGS pages. Fable drew the candidates on the Bandits summit at mesa scale (docs/expertise-landscape/markers/).

## Dropped by everyone
- Petroglyph on varnish and Moki (toe-hold) steps: Ancestral Puebloan visual language; using it to decorate a personal record is appropriation even when abstracted (Sol, Terra, Opus, research agent all independently). Also illegible at 6 px.
- Fins: a row of upright slabs is a bar chart and gives items the height the design reserves for terraces (all four).
- Hoodoos, balanced rocks, boulders: the mushroom family (all four).

## Ranked
1. Inscription cut into the lit cliff face below the bench, El Morro style: Opus first, Terra first, Sol third. The numeral is the mark; a straight-stroke type sign precedes it (square paper, square with diagonal patent, plus system, chevron post, dash note); a draft is scratched in the shade tone with a dashed stroke and no highlight. It uses the one surface that faces the reader in plan-oblique and leaves the bench clear. Risk: reads as a caption if the cut highlight and bedding gap are missing; needs the riser to be at least 10 px tall at the mesa zoom (ours is about 22).
2. Low cairn: second for Sol and Terra, third for Opus, first for the research agent. Three offset slabs, 7 px wide and under 6 px tall, no outline, no symmetric cap, the draft's top stone lying beside it. Honest Plateau trail language; risk of collapsing into a cone or a burger at 6 px.
3. Survey benchmark disk: Sol's first, fourth elsewhere. A plan-oblique ellipse set flush, rim nicks for type, an open ring for a draft. Exact in meaning (surveyed, recorded) but close to a chart dot unless the socket and rim are disciplined.
4. Tinaja: Opus second, Terra third, Sol fourth. The best draft metaphor (dry versus holding water) and native to slickrock, but it says erosion rather than work, and rows read as beads.
5. Iron concretion (research agent's addition): natural, legible, but carries no sense of authorship and little type.

## Recommendation
The inscription. It is the only option where the work is entered into the rock rather than placed on it, and it keeps the two rules that have held since round 3: no second height scale, and the numeral as the identity. Cairn is the fallback if the inscriptions read as labels in a real test. Type signs stay on a 5 by 5 straight-stroke grid and never borrow rock-art motifs. Method-page sentence (Opus): "Each piece of work is cut into the cliff below its bench the way travellers signed Inscription Rock at El Morro, with a plain mark for what kind of work it is and its number; a draft is scratched but not yet cut."

## One thing not to do
Do not turn the material types into pictograms. One physical mark, small straight-stroke variations, a documented code (all three voices).
