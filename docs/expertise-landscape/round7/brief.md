# Council round 7 brief: keeping each field whole, and joining fields only where they really join

You are one voice in a blind design council. Answer independently, concretely, in 1,400 to 2,000 words, with rules as pseudocode where asked. Think hard; this round changes the geometry rule that round 4 settled, at the owner's request.

## The object
A personal research site draws a "map of work": a plan-oblique relief of flat-topped mesas, one per field (Optimization, Bandits & learning, Robotics, AI systems, Cloud infra, Catan RL lab, Hyperspectral unmixing). Each mesa is a stack of terraces built from a ledger: a pale submerged shelf for the reading queue, then benches for works cited in his papers, works read, his own writing, and at the summit his published work. Each terrace's width follows the log of the count at that layer, and the terraces are cumulative (a published paper is also something he read, so the summit sits on the read bench). Fields that share ledger entries are joined; the shared entries build the land between them. Styles: Colorado Plateau strata colours; light from lower left; no textures beyond bedding lines.

## The round-4 rule, as built today
A shared entry counts at its own layer and at every layer below it (cumulative sharing). For each pair of fields, at each layer k, a capsule region between the two centres has half-width = (smaller field's terrace radius at k) × (shared cumulative count at k / smaller field's cumulative count at k). The layout then places two fields at distance r_A,k* + r_B,k* + 14, where k* is the highest layer at which the shared share is at least 20 percent. Round 4 ruled that arches are therefore impossible by construction and called that a feature: a joint publication builds a thin full-height wall from summit to summit, and "the map cannot show a connection at the top without a foundation".

## What the owner sees and objects to
1. When two fields share a publication (Bandits and Robotics; Optimization and Hyperspectral), the layout pulls the summits together and the smaller field's lower benches vanish inside the larger field's. Robotics' own cited and read entries, which have nothing to do with Bandits, lose their shape. In his words: "the links which are robotics will stay robotics, the blogs which are robotics-specific stay robotics-specific; if the blog is actually common between robotics and optimization, THEN and ONLY THEN should they be merging." He wants "the individual areas to keep their individuality".
2. A joint paper with no shared reading should, he says, be "an arch of sorts": connected at the top, nothing shared below. The cumulative rule denies this.
3. Separately, Optimization and Bandits look too far apart even though they share many cited and read entries; the cause found is the layout's repulsion rule using the reading-shelf radii (AI's 180-link shelf pushes Optimization away via Bandits).

## Proposed replacement (critique it, improve it, or replace it)
- Field terraces stay cumulative. Each field always draws its complete own stack.
- Sharing becomes exact-layer: shared entries at layer k build a span at layer k only. A span is a separate polygon (not part of the height grid): a capsule from field A's layer-k ring to field B's layer-k ring, drawn at height k with a one-step riser on its viewer-facing edge, rendered after the terrain, so whatever lies beneath it (the two fields' lower benches, or sea) shows under it. Width: half-width = r_small,k × min(1, s_k / c_small,k) with s_k the exact shared count at k and c_small,k the smaller field's exact count at k. A share of 100 percent makes the span as wide as the smaller terrace, so the two terraces fuse at that layer and only at that layer.
- Layout: land shores never overlap. Every pair sits at least r_A,2 + r_B,2 + gap apart (cited-terrace radii, not shelf radii), gap 10 for pairs that share anything and 36 for pairs that share nothing. Shelves may overlap in the water. Positions are then locked.
- Consequences to examine: a single joint paper between two otherwise unrelated fields becomes a long thin span from summit to summit, crossing over both fields' lower benches and the gap between them, one step thick: a natural bridge or arch. Two fields that share entries at every layer get a span at every layer, which stacks into a wall, so they read as one massif, which is right. Shared reading only gives a low saddle between the read benches with the summits apart.

## Questions
Q1. Is exact-layer sharing the right rule, and is "merge at a layer only when entries are shared at that layer" the right sentence? Where does it mislead, if anywhere? Consider: a field whose only published item is the joint paper (Robotics today: both its published items are bandit papers), spans that cross a field's own benches, spans between fields that are not neighbours in the layout, three-field entries, and what a reader infers from an arch versus a wall.
Q2. The span's geometry and drawing. Capsule from ring to ring versus centre to centre clipped; how thick (one step? thinner, like a slab?); how its riser and underside are drawn in flat plan-oblique with no cast shadows; whether the lower terrain shows unchanged beneath it or is dimmed; what happens where a span lands on a summit (same colour, no seam?) and where two spans cross; the minimum width at which a span is drawn at all (one shared entry out of forty?).
Q3. Layout. The land-shore rule above, the two gap sizes, how to order the stress relaxation so that the strongest sharing is shortest, and how to keep Optimization and Bandits adjacent when they share reading but no publication. Should the layout distance use the lowest shared layer (so that fields sharing cited entries sit base to base) or the highest?
Q4. Honesty and the method page. Write the sentence that replaces "the map cannot show a connection at the top without a foundation". Is there any case where the exact-layer rule lets the map claim something false?
Q5. The repository question. The owner asks whether the whole rendering (the Python builder that turns the ledger into terrain and the React component that draws it) should move to a separate repository as a generic component that the site calls, so that the rules are isolated and easy to verify. Assess effort against benefit for a one-person site: what a clean boundary would look like (input schema: fields, layers, materials, entries with tags and layers; output: terrain paths, spans, marker positions, labels), what tests would make the rules verifiable (golden fixtures: a toy ledger of three fields with known shared entries and the expected radii, spans and distances), and whether to do it now, do a cheaper version now (a self-contained folder with tests inside the site repo), or defer until a second site uses it.
