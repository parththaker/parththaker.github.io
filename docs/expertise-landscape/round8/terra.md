## A. Connector

Ranked choice: use per-layer masks as the construction method, add the arch riser only for joins that have no joined layer below, and do not use a cast shadow. Keep the deck one modest value step darker than its mesa’s terrace, not textured. The join should read as a landform when it is a shared ridge and as a bridge when it is a summit-only relationship.

For a join at layer \(k\), take the two nearest points on the two layer-\(k\) contour boundaries, rather than aiming toward either mesa centre. Sweep a variable-radius capsule between those points, overlapping each field’s layer-\(k\) mask by 6 px. Its end half-width is the already-defined semantic width; its middle half-width is 0.62 times that width. Ease from 1.00 to 0.62 and back with a cubic curve over the outer 35% of the span, rather than making a pinched diamond. Blur the union of the terrace mask and this capsule with a 5 px Gaussian before contouring at the same threshold used for the mesas. This gives a tangent-continuous fillet at the abutment instead of a straight slab corner.

Do not let the visual smoothing broaden the counted join. The semantic half-width remains the prescribed value before blur; the contour may move at most 3 px outside it. If that constraint cannot be held at a 3 px minimum join, list the relation as text rather than drawing a hairline connector.

The visible connection must stop at the nearest layer-\(k\) terrace boundary, with the 6 px overlap hidden inside that terrace. It must not continue to summit centres or through a terrace’s inner core. That is an honest shortening rule: it claims that the shared material reaches the common edge of two layer-\(k\) shelves, not that the fields have a shared central peak. It should substantially reduce long published-to-published bridges.

For an isolated summit join, use the published deck tone at 92% of its original lightness: roughly a muted version of `#DCCDB9`, not a different material. This preserves the layer meaning while ensuring the two summit decks remain the brightest surfaces. Do not add bedding-line texture; texture will look like a special object laid on the map and will be noisier than the absent item glyphs.

Give the near, viewer-facing long edge a fascia with a maximum visible drop of 0.35 step heights at both abutments. Its lower edge rises as a symmetric elliptical arch: it is 0.35 steps below the deck 10% in from each end, 0.12 steps below at one quarter span, and 0.04 steps below at the midpoint. Leave the region below that curved edge unfilled so the bench remains visible through it. The effect is a shallow natural bridge, not a dark tunnel. Draw the deck contour in a 1.1 px Tanaka edge stroke; use the normal light stroke on the west-facing contour and shade stroke on the east-facing contour. Draw the fascia’s lower arch edge at 0.9 px, at 60% opacity of the normal shade stroke. There should be no separate outline around the whole object.

When every layer is shared, render one such mask per shared layer. The layers should meet as a continuous terraced saddle: each terrace has its ordinary 12-unit plan-oblique shear, its normal west lit riser and east shaded riser, and its contour should travel continuously across the join. Remove the arch opening entirely in this case. Each riser is a full step high where the corresponding layer below is joined, because the shared material genuinely continues downward. There should be no crosswise seam down the middle of the ridge; only the terrace contours and normal tonal steps explain its structure.

For a summit-only share, there is one mask, one narrowed deck, and one shallow open fascia. The bench beneath remains fully lit and unmodified. I disagree with adding a cast shadow, even a narrow one. In this composition the shadow would still describe the bench as subordinate to a hovering object, repeating the rejected dimming problem at a smaller scale. The arch edge already supplies depth; the map’s Tanaka shading should remain its only terrain-lighting system.

One thing not to do is retain a straight-sided rectangle anywhere in the connector pipeline, including as a clipping primitive. A rounded cap alone will not fix the plank problem; the varying waist, tangent fillet, and contour-derived boundary are the necessary parts.

## B. Stack panel

Use an inline right panel on desktop once a field is focused. At the 1024 px maximum content width, use a 16 px gap, a map column of 60% of available width, and a panel of 40%; the SVG keeps its 900×520 viewBox and scales down rather than changing its internal geometry. In the unfocused state, the map returns to full width and the panel is absent. This preserves the focused field as the subject while keeping the surrounding mesas visible.

Below 768 px, put the same panel below the map, full width, immediately after it. Do not use a Sheet as the primary mobile interaction. A Sheet disconnects the records from the terrain being selected and makes a five-section stack feel modal. Preserve the current two-state camera: tapping a field focuses it, tapping the focused field or a clear “map” control returns to the overview. The panel scrolls independently only on desktop; on mobile it becomes normal page flow.

Order the stack from top to bottom as published, written, read, cited, to read. Each header is a full-width terrace band in its layer tone and contains the exact text `published · 4`, substituting the live count. A selected header gets a 3 px left rule in the site red and a 1 px red bottom contour; it is not merely bolded. On the map, the selected terrace receives a 10% lighter deck overlay and a 2 px site-red contour following its outer edge. Do not recolor the whole mesa or fill its risers red. Clicking a terrace opens and scrolls to its matching section; clicking a section applies that map highlight.

Published and written should be expanded by default. Each public item has its title, a mono ledger line such as `2022 · PAPER · NEURIPS`, and its existing destination link. Posts and projects use the same shape with their correct type labels. Read, cited, and to read should default to counts and short descriptive state, not public bibliographies. The default expand control for read and cited is exactly `show 12 linked records`; it appears only when the owner has explicitly marked those records public. Expanding reveals title, authors where available, year, and an outbound source link. This is the better compromise: the information architecture supports a public reading trail without silently making private reading history public.

The alternative is a separate, explicitly public bibliography page, linked from those controls, containing only curated records rather than every private read or citation. That avoids exposing abandoned leads, personal interests, and incomplete annotations. If neither public-record mode nor curated bibliography is enabled, the control reads `reading records are private` and is disabled. To-read should not expose titles by default either; it gets the same privacy setting.

Shared records in any expanded section carry the exact secondary line `shared with Robotics`, substituting the other field name. A join is its own interactive target. Selecting it focuses both fields without changing either count and opens a compact shared subsection titled `3 read entries shared with Robotics`. If public record expansion is enabled, the titles appear below that line; otherwise it says `reading records are private`. A shared published item must similarly appear under `1 published entry shared with Robotics`, not be inferred from the arch alone.

Remove the old 01–22 numbering. Keep the table below the map, but make it a research index rather than a legend: field, layer, title, year, and type are sufficient. Every row links to a stable location such as `#bandits/published`; individual public records append a slug, for example `#bandits/published/regret-minimization`. A join uses alphabetic field order, such as `#bandits--robotics/read`. Opening a hash focuses the applicable field or pair, scrolls the matching stack section into view, and applies the same contour highlight. This is a stronger connection than numeral matching because it remains meaningful in copied links and assistive technology.

Use these empty states exactly: `no published work in this field`, `no written work in this field`, `nothing read yet in this field`, `nothing cited yet in this field`, and `nothing to read yet in this field`. These describe the work record without narrating the interface.

Do not add hover cards containing miniature citations or metadata previews. With the marks removed, hover cards would recreate the old dense, map-first indexing system in a less accessible form.

Confidence: high. I would change the no-shadow recommendation only if testing shows the open fascia is unreadable at the smallest desktop map width; I would change the inline-panel recommendation only if focused-map interaction requires more than roughly 600 px of rendered SVG width to retain usable terrace targets.
