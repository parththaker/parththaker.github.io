Round 4 synthesis (Fable)

The owner's question: build the land from what he has actually read and written, in five layers (to-read, cited, read, written, published), and make fields that share an item link up by rule, with Grand Canyon, Bryce and Arches as the picture in his head.

Four blind voices answered the same brief: Fable, Sol (GPT 5.6 sol, xhigh), Terra (GPT 5.6 terra, high), Opus. Fable also built a prototype (sketches/strata/) on synthetic counts shaped like the real inventory and rendered four variants; three are reproduced in the dossier. Blindness note: Opus's three-line hand-back summary reached Fable before Fable's voice was written; Fable's rules were already in the prototype by then.

Where all four agree

  1. One layer per item, the highest it has reached. A cited paper later read leaves "cited" and enters "read"; nothing is counted twice.
  2. Terraces are cumulative. Terrace k is built from the items engaged at least that deep, so every terrace sits on the one below. Exact slabs were rejected by all four: they allow a top wider than its base (a ledger artefact, not a fact) and floating slabs.
  3. Log compression. Linear area would let a one-paper summit vanish under two hundred links.
  4. The to-read layer is a submerged shelf, not land. All four reached this independently. It is drawn under the water line, outside the label counts, listed collapsed in the index. The owner asked for a lowest layer; this is the lowest layer, and it is the only one that may be large without meaning anything.
  5. A shared item builds terrain, not a line. The connection is made of the shared items by the same rule as a mesa: its height is the deepest shared layer, its width grows with the log of the shared count, and it is never wider than the smaller mesa at that layer.
  6. Arches and windows are impossible by construction, and that is a feature. An arch is a connection above with nothing below; cumulative terraces cannot make one, and the claim it would make ("we share a publication but no reading") is one the map should be unable to make.
  7. The credential stays a seal, outside the terrain.
  8. Blocks survive only for layers 4 and 5, each seated on its own bench. Layers 1 to 3 are never drawn as hundreds of objects. (Opus alone wanted one engraved dot per item for layers 2 and 3; the other three reject it under the round-1 texture rule.)
  9. Cited stays below read, named "cited in my papers" and "read". The reader who notices is a professor, and will trust the page more for it. Imported bibliographies never promote an item to read.
  10. Positions are computed once from the shared counts, then locked. A new field is placed alone in free sea; relayout is an explicit new edition with a changelog line, never a side effect.
  11. Merge and split are warnings, never automatic. A tag outside the taxonomy (the congestion-game paper's "game theory") is a build error; the build never invents a field.
  12. Four things not to do, from four voices: no bulk import of bookmarks or browser tabs into the queue (Opus); "read" comes only from the ledger, never from a citation or a download (Sol); no tuning of constants or retagging after seeing the picture, the owner's own "never weaken a gate after seeing a result" (Opus); nothing weighted by venue, citations or importance (Fable).

Where they differ, and the rulings

A. Where the logarithm goes. Sol and Terra: terrace area is log of the cumulative count. Opus: terrace radius grows a fixed step per doubling of the cumulative count. Fable: each bench is as wide as the log of its own exact count, and radii are the benches summed outward. The prototype decided it. Under Opus's law (render v4) a four-paper summit is 27 px across while a forty-item cited bench is 17 px wide, because a bench's width then depends on the ratio of two cumulative counts, not on how many items sit on it; the mesas read as stacked plates with large tops. Under Fable's law (render v3) a reader can read a bench as "this many items at this depth", and the picture has distinct mesas with legible steps. Ruling: Fable's bench rule with Opus's minimum bench width; the terrace still means "at least this deep". Constants are fixed on the real data once and then locked.

B. The layout law and accidental fusion. Opus: shore distance shrinks continuously with the layer-2 overlap, shores touch past 43 percent. Sol and Terra: a hard threshold before two fields may fuse (3 shared items and 50 or 35 percent), because one joint paper must not swallow a small application field into a large method field. Fable: distance set by the highest layer at which the shared items are at least 20 percent of the smaller field's items at that layer. The prototype showed that hand placement makes capsules into scaffolding (render v1: six full-height walls), and Opus's continuous law packs everything into one lump with the fins hidden inside (v4). Fable's law produced the mesas, saddles and twin summits the owner is picturing (v3). Ruling: Fable's law, which already contains Sol's and Terra's safeguard: one shared paper pulls two summits together only when it is a large share of the smaller field's publications, that is, only when the smaller field is mostly the shared work (Robotics, both of whose papers are bandit papers). Below the threshold a shared publication remains a thin full-height fin between separated summits; lint warns when a fin's exposed span exceeds about 50 px so the owner can nudge the lock. Drawing stays continuous; thresholds live in layout and lint only.

C. Shipped systems: layer 5 or 4. Two to two. Fable and Opus: 5 when a third party has accepted the work (a referee, a patent examiner, or production users with a public talk or post). Sol and Terra: 5 means a paper or patent, so systems are 4. The owner's words put systems at 4. Recommendation: Opus's acceptance criterion, so systems with a public artefact are 5, because the heaviest reader in the visitor model (hiring manager, weight 20) must see a summit on the fields of the owner's current profession, and because every block is typed, so a system can never pass as a paper. This is the one decision left to the owner and it is one constant.

D. Live blog posts and the arXiv manuscript. Unanimous: posts on the site are layer 4 ("written": own writing short of acceptance). The manuscript is 4 until it is accepted (three of four; Fable had said 5 for a preprint and concedes).

E. Fields of cited items. Opus: a fetched reference inherits only the primary field of each paper that cites it, and two fields share a reference only when papers in both fields cite it (bibliographic coupling). Sol and Terra: inherit all fields with an "inferred" flag for review. Ruling: Opus, because otherwise the bibliography of one two-field paper becomes a thirty-item causeway.

F. Visibility defaults. All four want three values: public (listed and linked), counted (shapes the land and the counts, no title emitted), private (absent). Ruling by majority: to-read and read default to counted; cited is public (it already is, in the bibliographies); layer 5 must be public and lint rejects anything else; drafts and ideas default to counted ("+2 in progress"); confidential work is private. The claim line therefore says "every public item is listed; the rest are counted".

G. Cadence. Fable: regenerate on every build. Sol: weekly or with the release. Terra and Opus: a monthly dated snapshot with an immediate exception for a new publication. Ruling: the build is deterministic and runs on every deploy; the ledger is committed in batches at the owner's pace, which is the real cadence; the colophon prints the data date and "positions locked".

H. Items in three fields. Star junction from the centroid (Fable, Sol, Terra) rather than three pairwise bridges that can enclose a pool (Opus). Maximum three fields per item (Opus).

I. The overview label line. BANDITS & LEARNING · 2016–2022 · 4 PUBLISHED · 14 READ: name, years, then at most two numbers. A field with nothing published prints its highest layer instead. A field that is only shelf is unlabelled at the overview and listed in the index as submerged (Opus).

The verdict, as rules

  • Layer ℓ(item) = max over sources: published 5 (accepted by a third party: refereed paper, granted patent, shipped system with a public artefact), written 4 (posts, ideas, drafts, filed patents, systems without a public artefact), read 3 (ledger entry with a date), cited 2 (fetched from the bibliographies), to-read 1 (ledger).
  • Counts per field: exact n_k and cumulative C_k = Σ_{j≥k} n_j, over public and counted items.
  • Bench rule: w_k = 0 if n_k = 0, else w_min + w_unit·log2(1 + n_k); r_k = Σ_{j≥k} w_j. Outline = circle times a fixed angular distortion seeded by the field id. Prototype constants w_min 4 px, w_unit 5.2 px at scale 1.
  • Shelf: layer 1 is drawn flat at sea level in the sea colour lightened one step, with a dashed hairline edge, no riser.
  • Bridge: for each pair with shared cumulative counts S_k, a capsule along the segment between centres at every layer with S_k > 0, half-width ρ_k = r_small,k · min(1, S_k / C_small,k), so the saddle is the smaller mesa's terrace times the fraction of its items held in common. Three-field items build a star from the centroid.
  • Layout: k* = the highest layer with S_k / C_small,k ≥ 0.2; linked pairs sit at d = r_A,k* + r_B,k* + 14 px; unlinked pairs repel to r_A,1 + r_B,1 + 36 px; stress relaxation, then write landscape.lock.json and never move anything. New fields are placed alone.
  • Lint: errors on duplicate ids after normalisation, untagged or unknown-field items, more than three fields, a layer that dropped without a reason, a layer-5 item not public, a field without a lock entry (prints a proposal). Warnings on merge (S_3 / C_small,3 ≥ 0.5 with C_small,3 ≥ 5, or every publication of the smaller field shared), split (more than 40 items at read or above whose secondary tags form two groups), a fin longer than 50 px, a shelf beyond the frame, a to-read item older than a year.
  • Objects: uniform-height typed blocks for public layer 4 and 5 items on their own bench; inked top for the last twelve months; nothing drawn for layers 1 to 3; hovering a bench at the island stop prints its count and highlights its index rows.
  • Index: a five-row stratigraphic column per field with counts, the public items under each row, the shared-with line, and a provenance note.
  • Legend line: "Shallows: on my reading list. Steps, lowest to highest: cited in my papers, read, written. Summit: published. Each step is as wide as the number of items it holds, on a log scale."
  • Claim line: "The land is made of what I have read and written, by rule, not by judgment. Height is how far I have gone with a topic, not how much it matters. Every public item is listed below; the rest are counted."

1. The strata rule

Every item has one layer, the deepest it has reached: published 5, written 4, read 3, cited 2, to-read 1. A field has exact counts n_k and cumulative counts C_k = Σ_{j≥k} n_j ("items engaged at least this deep").

The eye reads the width of each bench, not the radius of each ring. So the rule sets the bench width from that layer's own count and stacks the benches outward:

w_k = 0                         if n_k = 0
w_k = w_min + w_unit·log2(1+n_k) otherwise          (w_min = 4 px, w_unit = 5.2 px at scale 1)
r_k = Σ_{j≥k} w_j                                    (radius of terrace k; nesting is automatic)

Log because counts run from 1 to a few hundred; w_min because one item must still make a visible step; cumulative sum because a terrace holds everything above it. Shapes are circles made organic by a fixed angular distortion seeded from the field id, so the outline never changes between builds unless the counts do.

Worked numbers. Field A, n = (180, 60, 15, 2, 1): w = (43.0, 34.8, 24.8, 12.2, 9.2), so r = (124, 81, 46, 21, 9). Field B, n = (0, 40, 8, 3, 4): w = (0, 31.9, 20.5, 14.4, 16.1), so r = (83, 83, 51, 30, 16). A and B have the same land footprint (81 versus 83 px) but B's summit is 1.75 times wider and carries four hoodoos, its written bench is wide, and it has no shallows at all; A has a 43 px shelf under the water and a one-block summit. A reads as a field being waded into; B as a field built on. That is the right reading and it comes from the rule, not from an adjective.

Nesting: cumulative. Exact slabs would let a terrace overhang the one below it (four published, one read: a ledger artefact, not a fact about the owner), would need unsupported risers, and would make arches possible. Cumulative terraces are always supported, the visible bench width still equals the exact count, and "engaged at least this deep" is the honest sentence for a region.

The to-read layer is drawn as shallows: the same sea colour lightened one step, bounded by a hairline dashed shelf edge (the 200 m line on a chart), no riser, under the water line. It is the lowest layer, as the owner asked, but it is not land. A hiring manager sees land only where there is cited, read or written work; a professor sees the shelf as the direction of travel. Neither can mistake intent for depth.

Placement on the scale: patents are 5 (examined and public). Shipped systems are 5 when a public artefact exists (talk, post, repository) and 4 otherwise, listed as "work I can describe, not show". The credential stays a seal on the AI Systems mesa; it is a certificate, not an item of reading or writing. Blog posts live on the site are 4: the owner's own writing short of review. An arXiv preprint is 5 (public, citable, has an identifier) with "preprint" printed in the index; that one is the owner's call.

Legend line: "Shallows: on my reading list. Steps, lowest to highest: cited in my papers, read, written about. Summit: published. Each step is as wide as the number of items it holds, on a log scale."

Claim line: "The land is made of what I have read and written, by rule, not by judgment. Height is how far I have gone with a topic, not how much it matters. Every item is listed in the index below."

2. The joining rule, the layout rule, and the merge rule

A shared item is in both fields' counts and also builds a bridge. The bridge at layer k is a capsule around the segment between the two centres, present for every layer at or below the item's layer (cumulative again), with half-width

ρ_k = r_small,k · min(1, S_k / C_small,k)

where S_k is the cumulative shared count and "small" is the field with fewer items at that layer. In words: the saddle is as wide as the smaller mesa's terrace times the fraction of that mesa's items held in common. At 100 percent the smaller mesa is absorbed at that layer; at 10 percent it is a neck. Merging is therefore not a decision, it is a continuous consequence of the data, and the owner never chooses it.

The prototype found the rule that the brief did not ask about. With hand-placed centres 200 to 300 px apart, the capsules become walls: a shared publication draws a five-step ridge from one summit to the other, straight across both mesas' benches, and six of them read as scaffolding, not geology (first render). Tightening positions by hand did not fix it (second render). What fixed it was making layout a function of the data:

k*(A,B) = highest layer with S_k / C_small,k ≥ 0.2
d(A,B)  = r_A,k* + r_B,k* + 14 px              for linked pairs
d(A,B)  ≥ r_A,1 + r_B,1 + 36 px                 for unlinked pairs (repulsion only)

solved by a few thousand steps of stress relaxation, then written to the lock file and never moved again. Two fields that share a publication now sit with their summits a notch apart (Bandits and Robotics become twin summits on one high plateau; Robotics' own cited and read benches wrap around below as its own skirt). Two fields that share only reading sit at the distance of their read terraces, joined by a wide low saddle (Optimization and Bandits). Two fields sharing nothing sit in open sea. On the owner's inventory every field shares something with Bandits or with AI Systems, so the result is one connected landmass with seven mesas, not an archipelago. That is true of his work and it is the Colorado Plateau: one plateau, dissected, the same five formations showing in every mesa. The vocabulary changes from islands to mesas; nothing else from rounds 1 to 3 changes.

Merge and split are lint, not geometry: warn "consider one field" when S_3 / C_small,3 ≥ 0.5 with C_small,3 ≥ 5; warn "consider splitting" when a field has more than 8 published items or more than 40 items at read or above and their secondary tags cluster into two groups. The owner decides; the map only shows.

Items in three fields build a star: segments from the centroid of the three centres to each centre, same width rule, so that m = 2 is the segment as a special case and no triangle of ridges encloses a pool that would read as a window by accident.

A new field is added by hand to the lock file; the build proposes coordinates that satisfy the distance rule against the locked fields and refuses to run until an entry exists. Relayout is an explicit command with a version bump, never a side effect.

Mesa and butte: yes, a field is a mesa; a small field with a tall summit is a butte (Hyperspectral). Saddle: yes, shared reading. Fin: yes, one shared publication between two mesas is a thin full-height ridge, but the layout rule keeps it short, so it appears as a notch between twin summits rather than a wall. Sol (GPT 5.6 sol, xhigh, via tandem second-opinion)

My recommendation is a cumulative, log-area terrain: the queue forms a submerged shelf; referenced, read, authored, and formally published work form four equal-height emergent strata. Shared items create stepped saddles between fields, while a separate overlap threshold determines when two fields visually fuse. Counts remain explicit in the index because the terrain should communicate structure, not act as a ruler.

1. Strata rule

Give every distinct item one highest attained level:

  1. queued
  2. referenced
  3. read
  4. authored
  5. formal

“Highest attained” prevents double-counting. A queued paper later read moves from 1 to 3; its history remains in the ledger. A cited paper recorded as read occupies level 3, while retaining its citation relationship.

For field f, let:

n_{f,k}=\#\{i:\text{field }f,\ d_i=k\}

and the cumulative population supporting stratum k:

N_{f,k}=\sum_{j=k}^{5} n_{f,j}

Use cumulative, nested footprints:

A_{f,k}=a\log_2(1+N_{f,k})

where a is one global area constant chosen to fit the locked layout. A deterministic, smooth unit contour derived from the immutable field ID is scaled to area A_{f,k}. Because N_{f,k+1}\leq N_{f,k}, every upper contour fits inside the lower one. The unit contour must not be hand-adjusted to flatter particular fields.

Log area is the right compression. Linear area makes one publication almost invisible beneath hundreds of queued links; radius proportional to count would be far worse. Log area preserves order and meaningful ratios while leaving the index responsible for precise quantities. Equivalent radius varies as:

r_{f,k}\propto\sqrt{\log_2(1+N_{f,k})}

Independent exact slabs would be more literal about per-level counts, but they would permit a large upper layer over a small lower one, requiring overhangs or arbitrary support. They also destroy the coherent terrain model. Cumulative strata create mesas, buttes, and saddles and express the defensible claim “this many items reached at least this depth.”

The queue

Level 1 should be a submerged shelf, not emerged land. The waterline falls between levels 1 and 2. A large queue then reads as attention or intended exploration, not expertise.

A hiring manager seeing a huge emerged island would reasonably infer experience. A professor might instead see an inflated bibliography or aspirational reading list. A pale underwater contour communicates potential reach without granting it the same status as engagement.

Non-paper work

  • An issued patent is level 5: it is a formal, public, attributable output.
  • A peer-reviewed published paper is level 5.
  • A live blog post remains level 4. “Published on the website” is distribution, not formal publication.
  • Notes, drafts, ideas, and an arXiv manuscript in preparation are level 4.
  • A public shipped system belongs at level 4, represented by its best public evidence. Do not count the system, its talk, and a recap post as three outputs unless they are genuinely separate authored artifacts.
  • Confidential systems are excluded from the public model, not represented by anonymous mass.
  • A professional credential is not an intellectual work and should remain a CV/index badge outside the terrain.

2. Joining and merging fields

For fields f and g, define cumulative shared items:

S_{fg,k}=
\#\{i:f,g\in fields(i),\ d_i\geq k\}

At every level where S_{fg,k}>0, construct a rounded corridor between the corresponding contours. Its target plan area is:

B_{fg,k}=b\log_2(1+S_{fg,k})

For clear span L, solve the rounded-capsule area equation for width w; approximately:

w\approx B_{fg,k}/L

The layout should keep connected fields close enough that one shared item remains visibly renderable. Each connector is cumulative and nested, so a paper shared at level 5 produces a complete stepped saddle down through the lower strata. Its height is therefore determined by evidence depth and its width by shared population—the same rule as an island, elongated to join two shores.

The connection is a causeway or saddle, not a decorative line. Nevertheless, a single shared item must not make two disciplines appear taxonomically identical. Use a threshold for visual fusion:

q_{fg,k}=\frac{S_{fg,k}}{\min(N_{f,k},N_{g,k})}

Fuse the contours into one broad landmass at level k only when:

S_{fg,k}\geq3\quad\text{and}\quad q_{fg,k}\geq0.50

Below that threshold, retain a visibly narrow neck and separate shore shapes. Thresholding is preferable to accidental fusion caused by field spacing.

Emit a “consider merging fields” warning when, across levels 2–5:

  • overlap is at least 70% of the smaller field at two levels;
  • one of those is the highest level occupied by both fields; and
  • at least three distinct evidence items support the result.

Do not merge automatically. A legitimate subfield can be almost contained in a parent field.

Emit a “consider splitting field” warning when a field has at least 12 items and its item-relation graph has two clusters that each hold at least 25% of its items, with fewer than 10% as many cross-cluster relations as within-cluster relations. Relations include shared secondary tags and citations. This is a review prompt, not a taxonomic verdict.

An item tagged with three fields is a hyperedge, not three independently counted pairwise bridges. Build one Y-shaped junction at a computed Steiner-like point, with one shared central terrace and three branches. Ordinary pairwise-shared items remain on their respective pair connectors. This prevents triple-counting a single work.

Initial positions should come from a deterministic weighted graph layout. One suitable weight is:

W_{fg}=\sum_{k=1}^{5}\alpha_k\log_2(1+S_{fg,k}),
\quad
\alpha=(0.25,0.75,1,1.25,1.5)

This stops a large queue from overwhelming established work. Run stress minimization, resolve label and contour collisions, then write centers and routes to the lock file. When a new field appears, place only it: use the weighted barycenter of its neighbors, then choose the nearest free slot. Existing fields never move. A full relayout requires an explicit new map edition.

The honest geological vocabulary is limited:

  • Strata: exact correspondence; the five evidence states are horizontal layers.
  • Mesas and buttes: broad or small surviving upper footprints.
  • Saddles and causeways: shared-item connections.
  • Fins: narrow, high shared-work ridges.
  • Shoals: queued connections below water.
  • Hoodoos: only loosely; tiny isolated upper stacks may resemble them, but no erosion process produced them.
  • Windows and arches: unavailable. The stacked 2.5D solid has no overhangs or holes.
  • Grand Canyon-style dissection: unavailable because the model has accumulation, not erosion.

Losing arches and literal hoodoos is a feature. Inventing geology unsupported by the data would return the map to illustration rather than record.

Terra (GPT 5.6 terra, high, via tandem second-opinion)

My recommendation is to make this an evidence map, not a competence map: vertical order records the strongest documented relationship to material; horizontal extent records how much material sits at each relationship. It should be possible to recompute every contour from a ledger.

1. Strata rule

Assign every item one highest verified relationship:

1 queue       = explicitly saved to read
2 cited       = cited, but not recorded as read
3 read        = recorded as read
4 authored    = non-paper authored work: posts, notes, drafts, documented systems
5 published   = published paper or patent

An item may have a history across levels, but it is represented at its highest current level. Thus, a cited paper later marked read leaves layer 2 and enters layer 3; it is not counted twice. This makes the five layers mutually exclusive, while the terrace footprints are cumulative.

For field f, let n[f,k] be the number of public items whose highest relationship is exactly layer k. Let:

C[f,k] = sum(n[f,j] for j in k..5)

C[f,k] is the number of items engaged with at least as deeply as layer k. Every higher terrace is therefore contained by the lower one.

Use area proportional to a compressed count, not radius proportional to count:

A[f,k] = A_min + A_unit * log2(1 + C[f,k])

The renderer converts that target area into a smooth contour generated from deterministic item kernels: each item gets a stable pseudo-random point from its ID hash inside its field’s pinned local coordinate system; a smoothed density contour is scaled to A[f,k]. The seed never changes when an item is added, so existing local forms do not jump. This preserves the “built from items” promise without rewarding a reading queue with a continent.

2. Joining rule

Treat field membership as a hypergraph. An item tagged bandits and robotics is not duplicated as two unrelated stones: it contributes to both field terraces and to their shared terrain.

For a pair of fields a,b, define:

S[a,b,k] = count(items tagged a and b with relationship >= k)

Build a shared contour at every level using the same compressed-area rule:

B[a,b,k] = B_unit * log2(1 + S[a,b,k])

Geometrically, it is a low saddle/ridge corridor between the two pinned island bases. Its centerline is the shortest collision-free route between their shorelines; its width is chosen so its plan area equals B. It is not a decorative line. It is terrain made only from shared items. At a layer where there are no shared items, the corridor stops.

Use a ridge rather than a causeway: a causeway suggests an externally built connection, whereas the connection here is common material. In the lower layers this will often look like a broad shoal or saddle; in upper layers it may resolve into a narrow fin.

Keep fields as two named islands by default. Merge them into one labeled landmass only when, on the public record:

S[a,b,1] >= 3
and
S[a,b,1] / min(C[a,1], C[b,1]) >= 0.35

The connection itself should vary continuously below that threshold; the label/topology decision should be thresholded. Otherwise one shared paper could accidentally fuse a small application field with a major method field. The current multi-agent-search, congestion-game, and hyperspectral papers create visible high ridges, but should not by themselves merge Bandits with Robotics, Bandits with Game Theory, or Optimization with Hyperspectral.

Emit a taxonomy review warning, never an automatic merge, when either condition persists for a release:

shared >= 8 items
and Jaccard(a,b) = shared / union >= 0.50

That says the two field labels may be aliases or one topic seen at two resolutions. Conversely, warn that a field may need splitting when its items form two internally coherent groups with low overlap, low shared-neighbour similarity, and no bridge exceeding 15% of that field. This is a prompt for editorial judgment, not a clustering result masquerading as taxonomy.

For an item in three fields, construct one three-way saddle around a Steiner-like central node, not three full-strength pairwise bridges. Apportion its bridge-area contribution across the three arms so one tri-tagged paper cannot count as three independent discoveries of relation. It still counts once in every field’s own tally, because it genuinely belongs to each.

Initial placement, before the lock file exists, should minimize weighted graph distance:

weight(a,b) = Σk wk * S[a,b,k] / sqrt(C[a,k] * C[b,k])
where w5 > w4 > w3 > w2 > w1

Use a constrained force/stress layout with minimum island separation, then manually inspect label collisions and lock every coordinate. Since coordinates are already pinned, do not rerun that layout. A new field is placed once in the nearest vacant sea region to its strongest neighbours, tested for bridge and label collisions, then locked. Existing fields never move.

The geology metaphor is useful only where it remains literal:

  • Strata: yes—nested relationship levels.
  • Mesas and buttes: yes—broad or narrow cumulative caps can arise from the count profile.
  • Saddles and fins: yes—shared-item terrain, respectively broad/low or narrow/high.
  • Shoals: yes—the queue shelf.
  • Hoodoos: only as a loose visual analogy for isolated output markers; do not encode them as terrain.
  • Windows and arches: no. They imply erosion, holes, or unsupported spans. The model has neither negative evidence nor overhangs, so drawing them would imply a story the ledger cannot support.

That loss is a feature. The map should have geological discipline, not geological special effects.

Opus (Claude Opus subagent)

Core move: height is ordinal depth of engagement, width is log count, nothing else is free. The owner sets taxonomy, tags and a few constants locked before the first render; everything else is computed.

1. The strata rule

Layer of an item is the highest layer it qualifies for: L(item) = max(qualifying layers). Cited and read counts as read. Each item counts once per field.

Layers. 1 to-read (drawn as shallows, not land) · 2 cited · 3 read · 4 own writing · 5 published. Rule for 5: a third party has accepted it (refereed venue, granted patent, system in production with a public talk or post). Site blog posts and arXiv preprints are layer 4: public, but nobody else has accepted them.

Nesting: cumulative. Terrace k is drawn from C_k = Σ_{j≥k} n_j, the items engaged at least this deep.

Footprint: radius on a log2 scale.

s  = 9 px     # width per doubling, locked
r0 = 6 px     # locked
w_min = 4 px  # a non-empty layer is always a visible step
for k in 5..1:
    if C_k == 0: no terrace k
    r_k = r0 + s*log2(1 + C_k)
    if n_k > 0 and k < 5: r_k = max(r_k, r_{k+1} + w_min)
    if n_k == 0:          r_k = r_{k+1}     # zero-width step: the riser doubles, a cliff
terrace_k(F) = { x : |x - c_F| <= r_k * rho_F(theta) }
rho_F(theta) = 1 + Σ_{m=2..4} a_m cos(m*theta + phi_m), a_m <= 0.12, seeded by hash(field id), locked

Risers are equal (existing ZS); the Gaussian summits go away.

Why log radius. Area-proportional is the cartographic default, but counts span 250 to 1: field A's plateau would be 7 px on a 63 px base and the queue would dominate the map. Log radius makes each step width s·log2(C_k / C_{k+1}): wide when that layer multiplies what sits above it. It is a log axis, and the legend says so.

2. Joining and merging

Shared counts. For each pair of fields, S_k(F,G) = #{items with L ≥ k tagged both F and G}. S_k never increases with k.

Citation tags are derived, not inherited wholesale. A fetched reference gets the primary (first) field of each of the owner's papers that cites it. A reference is shared only when papers in two fields both cite it (bibliographic coupling), so one two-field paper's bibliography does not become a 30-item causeway.

Bridge geometry (Q2a). The bridge follows the same footprint rule as an island:

for k in 1..5 where S_k > 0:
    b_k = beta * s * log2(1 + S_k), beta = 0.5, floor 3 px      # half-width
    bridge_k = capsule(c_F, c_G, b_k)
    terrace_k(F) ∪= smooth_union(bridge_k, k_smooth = 6 px)     # same for G

The height of a bridge is the deepest layer with a shared item. Its width is log shared count. Because β < 1 and S_k ≤ min(C_k(F), C_k(G)), a bridge is never wider than the smaller island at that level. Bridges are drawn only between Delaunay neighbours; other pairs are listed in the cards.

Real cases:

  • Multi-agent search (bandits + robotics): S5 = 1, a 9 px fin at full height. If robotics owns little else, it is a hoodoo tied to a mesa by a fin: exactly the truth.
  • Hyperspectral (optimization + unmixing): also a fin.
  • Congestion game (bandits + game theory): lint error, game theory is not in the taxonomy. The build never invents a field.

(b) One landmass: continuous, never thresholded for drawing. The layout computes a target distance between centres:

J = S_2 / min(C_2(F), C_2(G))                  # overlap (Szymkiewicz–Simpson) coefficient
D(F,G) = (r2_F + r2_G) * (1.15 - 0.35 * J)

When J > 0.43 the shores overlap: one landmass, two labels. Below that, two islands joined by bridges; the waist narrows smoothly. Thresholds belong only to warnings, which are human taxonomy decisions.

(c) Warnings (build output, not geometry):

  • MERGE? when S_3/min(C_3) ≥ 0.6 with min(C_3) ≥ 5, or when S_5 = C_5 of the smaller field and C_5 ≥ 2 (every paper of the smaller field is also in the larger).
  • SPLIT? when a field has C_2 ≥ 40 and the optional per-item topics keywords form two co-occurrence components that each hold at least 30% of the field's items. Also when over 50% of a field's items are shared with two other fields in disjoint sets: it is a bridge, not a field.
  • SUBMERGED (information only): a field with no land, meaning only to-read items. A bank under water, unlabelled in the overview.

(d) Items in three fields count toward all three pairs, with no special triple-junction object. Three bridges can enclose sea between them: a lagoon. Four or more fields is rejected.

(e) Layout. At lock time, positions come from stress-majorization (MDS) over the target distances D, with a minimum sea gap of 24 px for pairs with J = 0. Positions go to the lock file. A new field is optimized alone against the locked positions, and only empty sea cells are candidates. Nothing placed ever moves. Islands grow in place. Unrelated shores closer than 8 px are clipped at the bisector, with a warning. If the map outgrows the canvas, the build stops and asks for a new edition (rescale s, at most yearly, old edition archived).

3. Rulings on Q3

(a) To-read on an expertise page.

  • For: honest about direction, which a hiring manager in LLM systems wants, at no cost in false claims.
  • Against: intention is not competence; hundreds of links would make the largest landmass out of nothing done.

Ruling: to-read is shown as shallows, never land, at count-only visibility. A hiring manager reads "where he is heading"; a professor reads "the land is honest; the queue is under water".

(b) Cited below read: keep the order. Professors know they cite papers they skimmed; the admission reads as integrity provided a layer-3 entry needs a date and a one-line note. Legend names: "cited in my papers" and "read closely".

4. The island stop

No second height scale: everything is flush or uniform.

  • Layers 5 and 4: the typed blocks survive as the public individual items., uniform height, seated on their own terrace, shape as type, inked top as recency. Counted drafts get no block, only "+2 in progress" in the card.
  • Layers 3 and 2: a flush engraved stipple, one dot per item, placed by seeded blue-noise inside the stratum's ring, with the seed taken from the item id. Read items are filled dots and cited items are open rings, both in the existing riser grey. Countable, not a density; about 60 dots in 55,000 px² of zoomed ring stays legible.
  • Layer 1: no marks. The shallows tint plus a count in the card: "~180 in queue".
  • Bridges carry their shared blocks or dots.

6. Two things not to do

  1. Do not bulk-import bookmarks, Zotero or browser tabs into the queue. Hundreds of clicks are not intentions; the shelf would dwarf the land and the ledger would mean nothing on day one.
  2. Do not tune s, β, the thresholds or the layer rules after seeing the picture. Do not retag items to manufacture a fin either. His own rule, "never weaken a gate after seeing a result", applies to a self-portrait. Changing constants is a new edition with a changelog line.
